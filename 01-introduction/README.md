\# Introduction to Cybersecurity



\## What is cybersecurity?



Cybersecurity is the practice of protecting computers, networks, programs, and data from unauthorized access, damage, disruption, or theft.



Its purpose is to keep digital information and systems safe.



\## Why cybersecurity matters



Organizations depend on technology to store information, communicate, provide services, and run daily operations. A successful cyber attack can cause financial loss, privacy violations, service outages, and damage to trust.



\## Common examples of cyber attacks



\- \*\*Phishing:\*\* deceptive messages that try to steal information or make a person take an unsafe action.

\- \*\*Malware:\*\* harmful software designed to damage, disrupt, spy on, or gain unauthorized access to a system.

\- \*\*Ransomware:\*\* malware that encrypts files and demands payment.

\- \*\*Password attacks:\*\* attempts to guess, steal, or reuse passwords.

\- \*\*Social engineering:\*\* manipulating people into revealing information or bypassing security controls.



\## The cybersecurity mindset



A cybersecurity professional thinks carefully about risk:



1\. What needs protection?

2\. What could go wrong?

3\. Who or what could cause the problem?

4\. What controls can reduce the risk?

5\. How would we detect and respond to an incident?



\## Key terms



| Term | Meaning |

|---|---|

| Asset | Anything valuable that needs protection, such as data, devices, accounts, or systems. |

| Threat | Anything that could cause harm to an asset. |

| Vulnerability | A weakness that could be exploited. |

| Risk | The potential for loss or damage when a threat exploits a vulnerability. |

| Security control | A safeguard that reduces risk. |



\## What I learned



Cybersecurity is not only about tools. It combines technology, people, processes, risk management, prevention, detection, and response.
