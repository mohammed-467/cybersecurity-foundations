# Threat Actors

## 1. Introduction

A threat actor is an individual, group, or organization that intentionally attempts to compromise the security of information systems, networks, or data.

Threat actors target the three main principles of cybersecurity:

- **Confidentiality** — preventing unauthorized access to sensitive information.
- **Integrity** — preventing unauthorized modification or destruction of data.
- **Availability** — ensuring systems and services remain accessible when needed.

Threat actors have different motivations, resources, skills, and objectives. Understanding who attackers are helps cybersecurity professionals predict threats and build stronger defenses.

---

# 2. Types of Threat Actors

## 2.1 Script Kiddies

Script kiddies are individuals who use existing hacking tools, scripts, or malware created by others without fully understanding how those tools work.

They usually have limited technical knowledge and depend on automated tools found online.

### Characteristics:

- Limited cybersecurity knowledge.
- Use tools developed by experienced hackers.
- Usually target systems with known vulnerabilities.
- Often motivated by curiosity, entertainment, or gaining reputation.

### Example:

A person downloads a DDoS attack tool from the internet and uses it against a website without understanding networking concepts or the impact of the attack.

---

# 2.2 Cybercriminals

Cybercriminals are attackers who perform illegal activities mainly for financial gain.

They target individuals, companies, and organizations to steal money, valuable information, or resources.

### Common activities:

- Ransomware attacks.
- Phishing campaigns.
- Identity theft.
- Financial fraud.
- Credit card theft.
- Data selling on illegal markets.

### Main motivation:

**Financial profit.**

### Example:

An attacker infects a company's computers with ransomware, encrypts important files, and demands payment to restore access.

---

# 2.3 Insider Threats

An insider threat comes from someone who already has authorized access to an organization's systems, networks, or information.

Because insiders already have access privileges, they can sometimes cause significant damage.

### Types of insider threats:

## Malicious Insider

A person who intentionally abuses their access.

Examples:

- Stealing confidential information.
- Selling company data.
- Damaging systems.

## Accidental Insider

A person who unintentionally creates a security risk.

Examples:

- Clicking a phishing email.
- Sharing passwords.
- Misconfiguring systems.

### Examples of insiders:

- Employees.
- Contractors.
- Former employees.
- Business partners.

---

# 2.4 Hacktivists

Hacktivists are attackers motivated by political, social, or ideological beliefs.

Their goal is usually to spread a message, protest, or draw attention to a cause.

### Common activities:

- Website defacement.
- Data leaks.
- Distributed Denial-of-Service (DDoS) attacks.
- Publishing stolen information.

### Main motivation:

**Ideology or social/political goals.**

### Example:

A group attacks an organization's website to protest a decision or policy.

---

# 2.5 Nation-State Actors

Nation-state actors are attackers supported or sponsored by governments.

They usually have significant financial resources, advanced technology, and highly skilled teams.

Their objectives are often related to national interests.

### Common targets:

- Government networks.
- Military systems.
- Critical infrastructure.
- Energy companies.
- Communication systems.
- Research organizations.

### Characteristics:

- Highly skilled attackers.
- Large resources.
- Long-term planning.
- Advanced attack techniques.

### Main motivations:

- Espionage.
- Intelligence gathering.
- Political advantage.
- Strategic influence.

---

# 2.6 Advanced Persistent Threats (APT)

An Advanced Persistent Threat (APT) is a sophisticated, long-term cyberattack where attackers maintain hidden access to a target network for an extended period.

APTs are often associated with highly skilled groups, including nation-state actors.

### Characteristics:

- Advanced techniques.
- Stealth operations.
- Long-term access.
- Careful planning.
- Target-specific attacks.

### Objectives:

- Collect intelligence.
- Steal sensitive information.
- Monitor activities.
- Prepare future operations.

### Example:

An attacker secretly enters an organization's network and remains undetected for months while collecting confidential information.

---

# 3. Threat Actor Motivations

Threat actors may have different reasons for attacking systems.

| Motivation | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Financial Gain | Making money illegally | Ransomware payments |
| Espionage | Collecting secret information | Government intelligence theft |
| Political Goals | Promoting beliefs or causes | Hacktivist attacks |
| Revenge | Damaging an organization | Insider sabotage |
| Disruption | Interrupting services | DDoS attacks |
| Recognition | Gaining attention or reputation | Script kiddie attacks |

---

# 4. Threat Actor Comparison

| Threat Actor | Skill Level | Main Motivation | Typical Target |
|---|---|---|---|
| Script Kiddie | Low | Fun, attention | Easy targets |
| Cybercriminal | Medium to High | Money | Individuals and companies |
| Insider Threat | Variable | Revenge, money, mistakes | Organizations |
| Hacktivist | Medium to High | Ideology | Public organizations |
| Nation-State | Very High | Intelligence and strategy | Governments and infrastructure |
| APT | Very High | Long-term objectives | High-value targets |

# 5. Real-World Scenario

Imagine a hospital network containing patient records, medical systems, and financial information.

Different threat actors may attack the hospital:

### Script Kiddie

Attempts random attacks against the hospital website using downloaded tools.

### Cybercriminal

Sends phishing emails to employees and installs ransomware to demand money.

### Insider Threat

A hospital employee steals confidential patient information.

### Hacktivist

Attacks the hospital website to promote a political message.

### Nation-State Actor

Attempts to collect sensitive information for intelligence purposes.

### APT Group

Quietly enters the network and remains hidden for months while collecting valuable information.

Each threat actor has different skills, motivations, and methods of attack.

# 6. Defense Against Threat Actors

Organizations use multiple security controls to reduce risks.

### Authentication Controls

- Strong passwords.
- Multi-factor authentication (MFA).
- Identity and Access Management (IAM).

### Network Security Controls

- Firewalls.
- Network segmentation.
- Intrusion Detection Systems (IDS).
- Intrusion Prevention Systems (IPS).

### Monitoring Controls

- Security logs.
- Security Information and Event Management (SIEM).
- Continuous monitoring.

### Human Security Controls

- Security awareness training.
- Phishing simulations.
- Access management policies.

### Technical Maintenance

- Regular software updates.
- Vulnerability scanning.
- Backup systems.

---

# 7. Key Takeaways

- Threat actors are individuals, groups, or organizations that attempt to compromise systems.
- Different threat actors have different skills, motivations, and resources.
- Threats can come from outside and inside an organization.
- Understanding attackers helps cybersecurity professionals build effective defenses.
- Security requires multiple layers of protection, including technology, policies, and trained users.

# 8. Cybersecurity Career Connection

Understanding threat actors is an essential skill for cybersecurity professionals because defenders must think like attackers.

Security analysts study:

- Who may attack.
- Why they attack.
- How they operate.
- What defenses can stop them.

This knowledge supports careers in:

- Security Operations (SOC).
- Threat Intelligence.
- Incident Response.
- Penetration Testing.
- Digital Forensics.
